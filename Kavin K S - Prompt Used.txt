## 3. Main Project Requirements

Project Name: Business Deviation Analysis & Commentary

Develop an end-to-end business analysis application using the Philips Advertising and Promotion dataset.

The project should analyze Advertising and Promotion (A&P) spending across product units, countries and markets.

Business context:
- Approximately €650 million annual A&P spending.
- 18 product units.
- 21 countries.
- Monthly actual spending versus budget spending.

The main objective is to identify where the A&P money is being spent, understand deviations from budget and explain whether the spending is justified by sales performance.

## 4. Data Processing and Transformation

Use Python to process and clean the provided dataset.

The processing should include:
- Load the Excel dataset and its reference sheets.
- Clean missing and inconsistent values.
- Remove duplicate records.
- Join product, currency and market reference information.
- Classify countries into the appropriate geographic markets.
- Handle records belonging to the shared Business market.
- Convert local currency values into EUR for global comparisons.
- Calculate the required financial metrics.
- Generate transformed datasets and data-quality reports.

Currency conversion should use the provided exchange rates, where the exchange rate represents local currency units per EUR.

## 5. Financial Analysis and Business Logic

Use the following metrics:

- Advertising & Promotion Local.
- Advertising & Promotion Central.
- Total A&P Spend.
- Total Sales.
- Budget A&P Spend.
- Actual A&P Spend.
- Budget Sales.
- Actual Sales.

Exclude Other Selling Expenses from A&P calculations.

Calculate sales-adjusted expected spending using:

Expected Spend = Budget A&P Spend × Actual Sales / Budget Sales

Calculate real overspend using:

Real Overspend = Actual A&P Spend − Expected Spend

The analysis must not consider every budget overspend as a negative business outcome.

For example:
- If spending increases and sales also increase, the additional spending may be justified.
- If spending remains the same but sales increase, efficiency improves.
- If spending increases but sales decrease, investigate the deviation.
- If spending decreases while sales increase, identify the improvement in efficiency.
- If both spending and sales change, evaluate spending relative to sales performance.

Do not fabricate profit calculations because the dataset does not contain all the costs required to calculate actual profit.

## 6. Statistical Analysis

Perform statistical analysis to identify unusual spending patterns.

Include:
- Modified Z-score anomaly detection.
- Statistical significance testing.
- T-tests.
- Multiple-comparison correction using the Benjamini-Hochberg method.
- Monthly deviation analysis.
- Product-country performance analysis.

Identify statistically significant deviations and distinguish them from ordinary fluctuations.
